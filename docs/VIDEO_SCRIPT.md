# Mars Explorer — four-minute recording script

Record at 1440 × 900 with the API awake and the EONET feed loaded. Run the path without cloud model keys. The optional Google Earth map needs a configured browser key; the Earth explorer works with its OpenStreetMap fallback. Keep the cursor clear of the title until the first interaction.

| Time | Screen and action | Spoken track |
|---|---|---|
| **0:00–0:25** | `/`. Hold the textured Mars globe, then scroll to the Earth feed. | “Mars Explorer is a research storytelling console for a hypothetical walk across Jezero Crater. It brings NASA imagery, a small source-linked regional dataset, and a cited local assistant into one path from question to briefing. The Mars globe introduces the surface setting; the Earth events below are a separate live NASA feed.” |
| **0:25–0:50** | Click **Start 4-min tour**. Show `/survival` with its illustrative 3D terrain, then `/jezero` with the textured Mars globe and seed inventory. | “A Marswalk starts with constraints: water history, terrain, power, and communication. Jezero is the science setting because NASA describes an ancient lake and delta here. The terrain study illustrates a planning question; the globe gives planetary context. Neither the scene nor the seeded points certify a route.” |
| **0:50–1:15** | Use tour strip to `/eonet`. Filter **Severe Storms** or another populated category, click an event, show timeline and source card, close. | “This is NASA EONET, reporting natural events on Earth. Colors encode categories and the register refreshes from the public feed. Opening a record shows its date, geometry count, magnitude when available, and original sources. Earth coordinates remain on this Earth map; they never enter the Mars surface console.” |
| **1:15–2:15** | `/explore`. Show NASA Trek base layer and Jezero points. Skip coach if necessary. Click **Load demo route**, inspect the risk badge, toggle watch zones, show one point detail. | “Here the working surface is NASA Mars Trek. We sketch a hypothetical traverse and calculate distance and nearby seeded science points. The risk score is a heuristic that checks local watch polygons and route shape. It does not measure slope or certify safety. Each point opens its provenance, and the original NASA source is one click away.” |
| **2:15–2:40** | Open **Assistant**, ask “Why is this route scientifically interesting?”; click a citation, then `/traces`. | “The default answer is assembled from a small local corpus. No Claude or Gemini key is required. Citations open as inspectable records, and the trace page shows retrieval, evidence grading, and response assembly. A missing source is a limit to disclose, not a reason to invent a claim.” |
| **2:40–3:15** | `/ops`: point to **SIMULATED / NOT ROVER TELEMETRY**, then separate Earth refresh panel. Continue to `/briefing/preview`, download server PDF. | “These WebSocket ticks are explicitly simulated. They show how a live interface could behave without claiming a rover downlink. The adjacent EONET headlines are real Earth data, visibly separated. The briefing is deterministic: it carries route metrics, objectives, caveats, and citations into a server-generated PDF.” |
| **3:15–3:40** | `/targets`, open one target detail; `/hazards`, open one watch zone. | “The target register links each approximate Jezero point to a source. Watch zones are local illustrations used for the route method. They are non-certifying and cannot be read as an EVA or navigation decision.” |
| **3:40–4:00** | `/architecture` flow diagram, `/story`, `/science`; end on source register. | “The source boundaries are deliberate: Mars Trek for Mars imagery, EONET for Earth events, local seeds for the demo, and simulated ops labeled as such. Mars Explorer is a transparent planning and storytelling aid. Its value is that every conclusion has a visible source and every shortcut has a visible limit.” |

If an upstream feed is slow, keep the timer by showing its explicit loading state and continue. Before recording, verify `/health`, open `/eonet`, load the demo route once, and download one PDF. The [deployment checklist](DEPLOY.md) covers the production smoke.
